Step 1: ADC Sampling

Digitizes the analog RF front-end output at the configured sample rate. The B1I signal at 1561.098 MHz is down-converted to an intermediate frequency, then sampled. A higher ADC rate widens the Nyquist zone, capturing more of the band but increasing data volume and power. The spectrum shows the signal energy concentrated near baseband amid wideband thermal noise.

Step 2: Mixer & LPF

A digital mixer multiplies the sampled signal by a complex carrier (e^(−j2πfIFt)) to translate the B1I signal to baseband. A low-pass FIR filter then removes out-of-band noise and images. The LPF cutoff must be ≤ the decimated Nyquist rate to prevent aliasing in the next stage. A 2 MHz cutoff is ideal for B1I's ~4 MHz null-to-null bandwidth.

Step 3: Decimation

Reduces the sample rate by an integer factor M (e.g. 50 MHz → 4 MHz via M=12.5, implemented as a CIC or polyphase filter). This lowers the FFT size and SRAM requirements. If the LPF did not adequately suppress energy above the new Nyquist rate (fs/2), high-frequency noise folds into the baseband, raising the noise floor and degrading acquisition sensitivity.

Step 4: Correlation (FFT + Int)

Performs circular correlation with a local replica of the B1I NH+PRN code via FFT: FFT(received) × conj(FFT(local)), then IFFT. Coherent integration (Tc) sums magnitudes over one or more code periods before squaring, gaining SNR proportional to Tc but suffering sinc-loss if residual Doppler is non-zero. Non-coherent accumulation sums |correlation|² across multiple coherent blocks, providing further processing gain against squaring loss.
